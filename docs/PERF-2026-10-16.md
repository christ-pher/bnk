# Performance sweep, 2026-10-16 (branch `perf-sweep`)

The user's measure is `/opt/benchmarks/bnk_bench.py` (LocalMaxxing context sweep against the server: cold read,
decode at depth, follow-up turn). Their FP4-Local numbers at v0.3.0, `-c 16`: 4K read 1,186 tok/s, 16K 1,935,
decode 97-105, follow-up TTFT 1.2 s; the IQ3_S release on 10-15: 1,948 / 2,430 / 105-120 / 0.6 s. Local repro
without the server: `bnk pbench` through the scratchpad's `pb2.sh MODEL PAIRS` (the server's settings; token
files for 4k, 32k, 64k, 128k, 253k).

## What was found

1. **Follow-up turns were bimodal on both models: 600 ms or 1,150 ms.** The n-gram PLE table (27-29 GB) is read
   by rows at hashed positions out of a file mapping; a row whose page the page cache had dropped is a synchronous
   4 KiB disk read, and a new turn's rows are mostly new. The slow case appeared whenever the other model's files
   had displaced the table (a model switch, a conversion). It was not the 3-slot config (two runs at 3 slots: 1,160
   and 607 ms). Fix: the table is pulled into RAM and locked at load (5 s when cached; `BNK_PLE_LOCK=0` only
   advises it; `LimitMEMLOCK=infinity` in the service unit). Now 600 ms every time on IQ3_S.
2. **FP4-Local prompt reads are bound by the expert copies.** 26% of its experts live outside VRAM; a 4K read
   staged 20 GB per card over PCIe (every outside expert, once per chunk), 1.6 s of copies against 1.2 s of
   kernels per card, GPUs 64% busy (nsys, `BNK_PB_PROF=cold`). Residency is the lever: every GiB of VRAM returned
   to the expert cache is ~1.6% more experts resident, fewer copies per read and fewer CPU misses per decode step.
   Freed: the token embedding table (now in page-locked host memory, gathered over PCIe at a few KB per token:
   1.2 GiB on each card, the MTP stage had its own copy) and the BF16 output head (Q8_0 at load, 1.2 -> 0.65 GiB,
   half the bytes per head GEMV). Residency 72 -> 75 / 79%.
3. **Decode is the known launch-bound chain** (IQ3_S, nsys: 785 launches per forward, dense GEMVs 43%, experts
   28%, attention 9%; each GPU 40% busy with one conversation while the other takes its turn). Tensor parallel and
   the persistent per-layer kernel were measured against exactly this on 10-15 and lost; nothing new here.
4. **NVFP4 experts on the CPU** went through ggml's per-token dot product; a multi-row AVX2 kernel (`mdot`,
   4 tokens per weight read) is 1.45x faster at 4 tokens and bit-identical (`test_cpu_kernels`).

## Measured (4K pair: cold read, 603-token follow-up, 200 decoded tokens with drafting)

| | FP4-Local before | after | IQ3_S before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cold 4K read | 1,254 tok/s | 1,407 | 1,980 | 1,972 |
| follow-up turn | 1,132 ms (up to 1,650) | 918 | 600 / 1,160 | 601 |
| decode | 96 tok/s | 110 | 101 | 112 |
| experts resident per card | 72 / 72% | 75 / 79% | 100% | 100% |

IQ3_S greedy tokens are identical to master's.

## Tried and not kept

* `--stage-small-mib 256` and `--prefill-small 1024` (smaller buffers kept between prompts): +2 points of
  residency, but reads got slower (the buffers are borrowed back and refilled during every read).
* A lower CPU cost in the prompt path's PCIe-vs-CPU split (`BNK_CPU_US_PER_EXPERT_TOKEN`, env override added):
  46 -> 20 -> 10 µs made the FP4 follow-up 914 -> 946 -> 1,035 ms. The CPU does not pay for short turns.
* Draft length 3 / 5 and cutoff 0.6 against 4 / 0.8 on FP4: within noise (105-111 tok/s).
* Two-chunk prompt groups (both chunks of a pair through each layer, a layer's outside experts staged once per
  pair instead of once per chunk; `BNK_PF_GROUP=1|2`): implemented (chunk-sequential per layer, so the
  recurrent, conv and KV states stay in order; greedy tokens identical with and without), measured slower in three
  interleaved repeats: 4K cold read 3,280 against 2,930 ms (-12%), 32K 14.4 against 13.7 s. The copies of one chunk
  overlap the compute of the previous one well enough already; what the pair saves in copies it loses in the next
  stage's later start. Removed from the branch; the design is in this note should residency ever drop further.

## What remains for FP4-Local

Its prompt path is bound by the 21-25% of experts outside VRAM: the PCIe copy engine runs at the link's 12.9 GB/s
during reads, and a short turn pays ~0.75 s of copies per card before anything else. Each GiB of VRAM returned to
the cache is worth ~1.6% of residency; what is left to take is small (the 2 x 0.6 GiB staging buffers, the 1.3 GiB
short-turn arena: both measured as net losses when shrunk). The honest ceiling for this model on two 32 GB cards is
therefore where it is now unless the model's weights shrink or a card is added.
